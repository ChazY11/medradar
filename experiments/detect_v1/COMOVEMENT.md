# Do competitors become active in the same technology at the same time?

Generated by `write_comovement_report.py` from `comovement.json`. No number below is typed by hand.

## Question

The single-cell detector answers "is this firm's activity in this track changing?" The Observe layer also needs the industry question: **are several independent firms moving into the same technology track at the same time?** That is a stronger and different claim about the industry than one firm accelerating.

## Design

* Panel: binary presence per (entity, track) month — 1 if the entity filed at least one material event in that track-month — over 119 complete months.
* Eligibility: at least 6 active months per entity and at least 3 eligible entities per track.
* Statistic: `T = sum over months of C(active firms, 2)` — how often firm pairs overlap in the same track-month.
* **Primary null**: each firm keeps its own number of active months and draws them with probability proportional to **the track's own monthly event volume**. This preserves (a) each firm's activity level and (b) the track's growth and seasonality, so only the cross-firm timing pattern is free. 
* **Secondary null**: the same but weighted by **lane-wide** volume only — reported to show how much apparent co-movement is really track growth.
* 1000 permutations, seed 7, Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.1.

**0 of 32 tracks show excess co-movement under the correct null.** For comparison, the lane-wide null — which does *not* absorb track-level growth — rejects **3** tracks: ivd_molecular, medical_ai, spine.

## What this means

Once a track's own growth is absorbed, **there is no measurable cross-firm synchronisation** in this lane. The three tracks that looked synchronised under the lane-wide null — ivd_molecular, medical_ai, spine — are exactly the tracks that grew most over the decade, so what looked like "firms moving together" was a growing track admitting firms over time. That is a specification lesson as much as an industry finding: a co-movement statistic is only as good as the volume profile it conditions on.

So the answer to the mission question is **negative for month-level synchronisation**: in this data, technology activity is essentially firm-specific, and claims that "the industry moved into area X" are not supported by co-timing once track growth is accounted for. Firm-level activity and track-level growth are the two phenomena present; their interaction is not.

## Limitations (stated, not hidden)

* **The negative z-scores must not be read as anti-synchronisation.** Under the track-volume null most tracks sit below expectation (down to about -3.87), because the null draws a firm's active months independently from a volume profile while real firms file in contiguous bursts. The test is therefore *conservative in the positive direction*: it can miss synchronisation, which makes the null finding strong, but its negative direction is an artefact of firm-level burstiness.
* Presence-based co-activity ignores magnitude. Two firms filing once and twenty times count the same in a given month.
* Month is coarse: firms entering the same quarter without overlapping a month are not counted as co-active.
* The lane's product-code taxonomy is unversioned, so a cell's membership can change for reasons unrelated to firm behaviour (see `track_assignment_audit.json`).
* This is a statement about the timing of *public regulatory activity*, not about internal R&D portfolios, and no causal mechanism is identified.
