# Cycle 3 — OBSERVE layer: can a technology change be detected early and honestly?

Generated by `experiments/detect_v1/write_report.py` from the artifacts in this directory. No number below is typed by hand.

## 0. Verdict

**The simple baseline is not beaten, and that is the result.** On the primary panel, inside a matched false-alarm budget of 2% per stream, the naive rate-multiplier alert detects 0.223 of injected sustained rate changes, while the best likelihood detector reaches only 0.181 (Poisson CUSUM) and the Poisson Bayes factor 0.122. The paired, entity-clustered differences versus the naive baseline are **negative**:

| detector | Δ detection vs naive | 95% clustered CI | excludes zero |
|---|---:|---|---|
| poisson_bayes_factor | -0.101 | [-0.134, -0.067] | True |
| poisson_cusum | -0.042 | [-0.070, -0.012] | True |
| rolling_zscore | -0.111 | [-0.153, -0.070] | True |

So the programme's recurring lesson repeats one layer down: **a well-matched simple test is hard to beat**, and buying complexity before fixing data density buys nothing. Absolute detection is low in every case — even the best detector misses most 2× shifts — and the binding constraint is the sparsity of the lane, not the algorithm.

**A caveat that constrains how far this can be pushed.** Injected shifts are abrupt, permanent, multiplicative changes to a Poisson rate. That is precisely the alternative a matched rate-ratio test is optimised for, and it is also the model in which CUSUM theory expects a likelihood detector to win. The injection therefore works as a **positive control**, not as a falsification test of the likelihood approach: the finding is narrow and is stated narrowly — *for abrupt multiplicative step changes on this lane, the simple test is at least as good*. Ramp, transient and heterogeneous alternatives are untested (backlog B-026). A full design review with kill conditions is in `docs/research/DETECTION_DESIGN_REVIEW.md`.

## 1. What this cycle was for

The mission was realigned to Observe → Understand → Anticipate, with technology-change detection as the new P1 behind the scientific-reliability floor P0; the forecasting programme was preserved at P3. This cycle builds the OBSERVE layer's first real capability: detecting a sustained change in a company's rate of technology activity on sparse monthly counts, with false alarms controlled and stated. The design was pre-registered in `research/DECISIONS.md` D-013/D-014 before any result existed, and two harness defects found during execution are disclosed in D-015/D-016.

## 2. The data, and why it constrains everything

Material events (510(k) clearances plus original / PANEL-TRACK PMA) from the frozen research lane, grouped by the deterministic legal-entity universe (no fuzzy matching) into monthly counts over 2016-11 … 2026-09.

| granularity | cells | entities | groups | events | median events/cell | median zero months |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| entity × track (primary) | 104 | 94 | 26 | 3337 | 25 | 97/119 |
| entity × track, deterministic track only | 98 | 88 | 24 | 3174 | 25 | 97/119 |
| entity × domain | 121 | 112 | 18 | 4093 | 25 | 97/119 |

Eligibility (pre-registered): ≥18 material events and ≥8 active months per cell.

A typical analysable cell has ~25 events over 120 months and is zero in ~98 of them: about **0.2 events per month**. A 2× rate increase moves the expectation from 0.2 to 0.4 events per month, which no single month can distinguish from noise. This is why detection delay and detection probability dominate every table below.

## 3. Noise control: is the technology dimension itself trustworthy?

Track assignment provenance, audited before the benchmark: **35693 of 37782** events (94.5%) get their track from the deterministic `{product_code: track_id}` map; **2089** (5.5%) come from device-text keyword matching. All 72 product codes that land in more than one track are in that fallback set. Keyword assignment is not deterministic per code, so a wording change can move events between cells and manufacture an apparent change — which is why a deterministic-track-only sensitivity level exists.

Second check: 1 of 410 cells with ≥12 events replace their product-code composition completely between their early and late halves, so wholesale relabelling is rare but not absent.

## 4. Benchmark design

* Real monthly stream shapes preserved; a sustained rate multiplier (2.0, 3.0, 5.0×) applied at onsets [36, 54, 72, 84]; post-onset counts re-drawn from a Poisson law at the injected rate, so injected streams are as noisy as real ones.
* A detection is credited inside [onset − 1, onset + 12] months. The one-month pre-onset tolerance is applied identically to every detector so that a detector reporting its estimated change point is not penalised for landing just before the truth.
* Cells are split by entity hash into halves; **all reported numbers come from the held-out half** and no entity appears in both.
* Injection seeds depend only on (cell, onset, multiplier), never on the detector, so every detector scores the **identical** injected series. Comparisons are therefore **paired**, and their intervals resample **entities**, not trials.
* The **entire false-alarm/detection frontier** is reported for every detector, one point per sensitivity knob. No single threshold is hidden behind a calibration step (an earlier single-point calibration proved unstable on ~50 cells — DECISIONS D-016).

Detection is respectable only for large shifts. A 2× change in this lane is close to undetectable at an honest alarm budget regardless of detector — which is a statement about the data, not only about the algorithms.

## 7. Spike versus sustained change

A single-month spike was injected with the same multipliers; declaring a spike to be a sustained change is the operational false positive that matters.

| detector | spike declared at 2× | at 3× | at 5× |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| naive_multiplier | 0.009 | 0.017 | 0.039 |
| poisson_bayes_factor | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| poisson_cusum | 0.000 | 0.004 | 0.009 |
| rolling_zscore | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

**Inconclusive, and reported as such.** On a ~0.2 events/month cell a one-month multiplier usually adds zero or one event, so the injection itself is barely distinguishable from the cell's own noise. The low rates therefore partly measure a weak injection. A real spike-versus-level-shift test needs denser streams (quarterly aggregation or larger cells) and is backlog B-026.

## 8. Real data: alert volume, FDR control, and confirmation

Over 104 eligible cells, a single pre-declared per-cell test (last 24 months vs the preceding 48 months, exact one-sided Poisson) rejects **17 cells** under Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.1 (cut-off p = 0.01603).

| detector | alerts on the full panel | alerts that also survive BH | novelty share (alerted) | novelty share (control) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| naive_multiplier | 0 | 0 | — | 0.000 |
| poisson_bayes_factor | 2 | 2 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| poisson_cusum | 0 | 0 | — | 0.000 |
| rolling_zscore | 1 | 1 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

Two honest readings. First, the **alert volume is tiny** (0–2 alarms across 107 cells and a decade) because the operating point is deliberately conservative — the detector is a needle-finder, not a monitoring feed. Second, the **objective confirmation signal failed**: the share of post-alert product codes that are new relative to the previous three years is zero in both alerted and control cells, because most cells exercise only one or two codes. This check cannot discriminate at this granularity and is recorded as a failure, not as evidence that the changes are unreal.

## 9. Negative results and what did not work

- **The likelihood detectors did not beat the simple baseline** on the primary panel (all paired differences negative, intervals excluding zero). This contradicts the hypothesis that motivated the cycle and is the cycle's main finding.
- **Poisson CUSUM is not usable at an honest alarm budget.** It needs a threshold of 40 before its false-alarm rate is acceptable, at which point it detects ~17 %. At looser thresholds it reaches ~50 % detection but alarms on ~43 % of clean streams.
- **No detector detects small (2×) changes** in this lane at any honest budget.
- **The spike-versus-level-shift test could not be evaluated** because injected spikes are not distinguishable from baseline noise at this density.
- **The novelty confirmation signal is uninformative** at this granularity.
- **A single-point calibration design failed and was replaced** by a full frontier (DECISIONS D-016); the superseded numbers are recorded there.

## 10. Limitations

- Injected step changes are **not** real technology changes. The benchmark measures detection of a statistical rate shift; it does not establish that a detected shift is strategically meaningful. That is an Understand-layer judgement.
- Because injections multiply the baseline rate by a constant, a matched rate-ratio test is the *correct* model for this alternative. The naive detector's win is therefore partly a property of the injection design — real technology changes may ramp or be heterogeneous, which is exactly the regime where likelihood detectors are expected to help. The honest conclusion is narrow: **for abrupt, permanent, multiplicative shifts on this lane, the simple test is best**; ramp and heterogeneous alternatives are untested (backlog B-026).
- Cells are treated as independent; correlated company-wide or regulator-wide surges are not modelled, so real-world false alarms may exceed the measured rate.
- Uninjected cells can contain genuine level changes, so the measured false-alarm rate is an **upper bound** on detector noise (noted by the detector implementer).
- Real-data confirmation has **no independent ground truth**: both regulatory pathways come from the same filing system, and the novelty signature failed.
- Track assignment is deterministic for ~94 % of events; the 6 % keyword fallback is a real false-change channel. The clean variant shows the ranking is unchanged, not that the channel is harmless.
- The lane is left-censored at 2016-11; the taxonomy is not versioned by effective date.
- **The final calendar month is excluded** because the lane ends 2026-10-15 and a partial month is about half its normal volume, which manufactures an apparent drop. Even so, a lane-wide volume artifact is present at the boundary of the original data and was flagged by the independent design review; detectors are not seasonally adjusted (January runs about 14 % below the annual mean), and their false-alarm rates therefore include real artifact, not only statistical noise.
- **The naive comparator is undefined on a large share of the data in principle**: most stream-months have a zero trailing-12-month baseline. The shipped implementation guards this with a minimum observed-event rule, but any rate-ratio alert is fragile exactly where this lane is sparsest, and the comparison should be read with that in mind.
- **'Material' is not 'technical'.** About 15 % of the lane's 510(k)s are 'Special' 510(k)s, i.e. modifications of the filer's own existing device rather than new technology. No filter separates them here, so a detected activity change may reflect device maintenance rather than a technology shift.
- **New product codes create churn.** Some recently created product codes account for a large share of recent activity in specific tracks, and a code that was unmapped earlier falls back to keyword assignment. This is a taxonomy-churn channel that can look like technology entry; it is documented by the design review and not yet controlled (backlog B-032).
- **No independent confirmation source exists in the current data spine.** The two regulatory pathways share a filing system, procurement rows are undated with `pit_usable=0`, publication counts are annual and cover 51 of the cohort entities, and there is no trials table. The 'is the change real' question is therefore **blocked, not answered**, and the design review marks it so.
- A detection is a statement about the rate of public regulatory activity. It is **not** evidence of intent, capability or internal R&D.
