## 1. The correction first: cycle 9's coverage conclusion was partly my bug

Cycle 9 reported that the Wayback hiring proxy covered only 2 usable companies and blamed archive depth. That was **wrong**. The collector combined `collapse=urlkey` with `limit` + `page` pagination; that combination does not page on this endpoint, so every query silently returned one capped page and most of the archive was invisible.

## 5. What is now established, and what still blocks

**Established.** The hiring lane is lawful, keyless and historical; it now covers 6 firms with ≥24 months of posting data; the collector's truncation is recorded per host rather than hidden; and the posting-URL shape audit is stored for platform-migration detection.

**Still blocking a verdict:**

* **Statistical power.** 3 usable companies cannot support a lag inference at any correction level. More firms must clear the floor, not a looser floor.
* **Platform migrations.** `shape_first_seen` is recorded per company; a switch between posting-URL templates creates a level shift that is a collection artifact and must be excluded before any level-shift claim.
* **Missing firms.** Philips, Dräger, Mindray, Zimmer Biomet and Masimo still return no archived postings under the probed hosts — including two of the five mandated focus companies.
* **Truncation.** Medtronic, BD, Boston Scientific and Stryker hit the per-host cap, so their series are lower bounds.

## 6. Verdict

This report is **not** a science result and does not pretend otherwise. It is the correction of a measurement bug that had produced a false conclusion ("coverage too thin"), plus a much better coverage map and a pre-registered test that is honestly reported as unrun. The B-030 lag question needs more *companies*, not a better estimator — and the remaining blocker is now specific: career-board discovery for five firms, of which two are mandated focus cases.
