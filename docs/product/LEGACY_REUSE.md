# Legacy Reuse Assessment

PRODUCT-001 required evaluating the accumulated research branches and draft PRs, then making an
explicit decision per component: **REUSE / REFACTOR / REPLACE / RETIRE**. This document records
what was actually inspected, the decision, and why. It is written after the build, so the decisions
reflect what was implemented, not a plan.

Review depth is stated honestly: some components were read in full (PR #12's web app, product store,
alias registry, the merged evidence/governance modules), others were assessed from their file
inventory, PR description and acceptance evidence.

## 2. Why not simply merge the old PRs

* #12/#13/#14 are **stacked drafts** whose base predates the current `main` by many merges; merging
  them would import a parallel product layer, a research-database dependency and thousands of lines
  the product does not need, while conflicting with the current `main`.
* Their product layer is bound to `data/medradar.sqlite` produced by the research pipeline. A
  product whose core data is regenerated by a research pipeline cannot offer a reproducible snapshot,
  a coverage statement or a stable acceptance bar.
* #13/#14 are observatory/reliability products whose core claim (continuous observation) PRODUCT-001
  explicitly does not make. Merging them would make the release claim more than it can prove.
* The scientific branches (#28/#29/#30 and the study PRs) are valuable research records; their
  results are frozen and must not be disturbed by a product merge.

## 3. What was concretely reused

1. **Reviewed alias lists for Dräger and Mindray** from PR #12's
   `medradar/product/entity_aliases.json`, with their reject list and decision notes, ported into
   `medradar/product/identity/organizations.json`. Provenance is recorded in the registry entry
   (`metadata_source`), and the review state remains `pending_review` because no human signed off.
2. **The applicant-attribution concept**: exact-string matching, accept/reject lists, per-record
   attribution metadata, provisional labelling. Re-implemented and strengthened (load-time
   integrity checks, duplicate detection, documented exclusions).
3. **The comparability framing** from the PR #12 statistical audit: complete-calendar-year windows,
   per-source coverage, pathway mix, "records are not innovation". Re-implemented as machine-readable
   `warnings[]` in the compare payload and rendered above the numbers.
4. **The FastAPI + static-SPA deployment shape** (one process, same-origin, no CORS) - kept because
   it is the right maintenance trade-off for a single developer.
5. **The research roster** (`config/companies_device.json`) as the base identity layer, extended with
   six additional non-US manufacturers to test search generalisation and with the two roster-absent
   acceptance firms (Dräger, Mindray).

## 4. What was deliberately not reused

* The `medradar/product/store.py` measurement code: it is correct for its purpose but is coupled to
  the research SQLite schema, the five-featured-firm model and a mutable dataset. The new
  `dataset.py` is smaller, snapshot-based and unit-tested against the committed data.
* The vanilla-JS frontend, its hash router and its component conventions.
* The observatory ledger/scheduler/backup modules (drafts #13/#14).
* Any claim of live operation, continuous validation or predictive signal.

## 5. Risk accepted

Porting concepts instead of code means the old acceptance evidence for #12 does not transfer to this
product. That is why this release ships its own test suite, its own end-to-end browser walkthrough
and a data spot-check against the live openFDA API (see ACCEPTANCE.md). The old PRs remain open as
historical records; they are not merged, closed or rewritten by this work.
