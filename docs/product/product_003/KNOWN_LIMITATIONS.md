# KNOWN_LIMITATIONS (PRODUCT-003)

Statements about this release, not a backlog of promises.

## Identity and relations

1. **A device identity is application-scoped.** `device:openfda_510k:K...` means "the device model this
   clearance covers"; `device:openfda_pma:P...` means "the device this approval covers". MedRadar does
   not claim to know the commercial SKU, the catalogue number or the UDI.
2. **No 510(k) product families.** openFDA exposes no family or predicate identifier for 510(k)
   submissions, so 12,000+ 510(k) devices have no family and the product says so. Grouping by name
   would be a candidate and is presented only as a candidate.
3. **Predicate relationships are not modelled.** No predicate identifier exists in the data and the
   510(k) summary documents were not collected. Predicate is not lineage anyway.
4. **14,742 devices have no relation at all** beyond their own application. That is a property of the
   source, not a defect that can be closed by inference.
5. **A PMA family is a regulatory hierarchy, not a commercial brand family.** The trade name field can
   list many models; the page shows them as recorded.
6. **56 PMA numbers appear only as supplements** because the original approval is not in this snapshot.
   They are labelled; nothing is fabricated for the missing original.
7. **Recall records do not identify devices.** A recall is linked only through the official
   `k_numbers` field. 8,576 recalls have no such field and are unlinked; 6,227 reference applications
   outside the snapshot.
8. **Candidates are hypotheses.** 21,881 candidate relations are generated by deterministic rules.
   They are labelled and never counted as established. No candidate is auto-promoted.
9. **No device relation accuracy estimate.** No independent sample of device relations has been
   reviewed, so the quality report returns UNKNOWN for accuracy. Rule consistency is not accuracy.
   Human review count is 0.

## Data and provenance

10. **Snapshot-derived only.** The device index is built from the same committed openFDA snapshot as
    the rest of the product; a new snapshot rebuilds everything (identifiers are stable per official
    number, but relation coverage will change with the data).
11. **No UDI/GUDID, no EU MDR, no NMPA, no clinical trials, no patents** for device identity. These are
    not collected and not claimed.
12. **The applicant is a record claim.** Manufacturer of record and current ownership are shown through
    the PRODUCT-002 attribution and PRODUCT-003 historical-owner model, never invented on the device
    page.
13. **Device names are not unique.** 446 normalised names are shared by different applicants; the
    product reports the ambiguity and never merges.

## Product and engineering

14. **First device request in a process builds the index (~4.7 s).** It is lazy and cached; the
    organization and event pages never pay it. The response exposes `index_build_ms`.
15. **Chromium-only browser acceptance**, no visual-regression baseline, no dedicated accessibility audit.
16. **Exports are CSV and Markdown** (device list CSV, device brief Markdown); no Excel-native or PDF.
17. **The device explorer caps pages at 100 rows** and sorts by relevance/date/name only; there is no
    full-text ranking beyond substring matching on names, applicants, models and official numbers.
18. **Alpha is local.** No hosted deployment and no external user has tested PRODUCT-003; the
    acceptance evidence is internal simulation plus browser automation.

## Governance

19. No new data source or committed data artefact was added for PRODUCT-003, so the GOV-0005
    compressed-binary exception is untouched. The licence gate and inventory still pass unchanged.
20. The applicant-attribution and historical-ownership semantics remain those of PRODUCT-002 and its
    P0 fixes; device pages display them rather than reinterpreting them.
